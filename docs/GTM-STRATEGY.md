# Neighborly GTM Strategy

## Strategic rule

Neighborly builds density before geography. The unit of strategy is a **geography × category micro-market**, not Canada as a whole. No starting geography or category is assumed; selection follows [the evidence framework](../marketing/strategy/MARKET-SELECTION-FRAMEWORK.md).

## North star

Optimize for successful local matches and completed local transactions. GMV, match rate, search-to-booking conversion, time to match, supply utilization, active providers, repeat rates, cancellations, disputes, referrals, contribution margin, and liquidity by geography/category explain marketplace health. Downloads, registrations, impressions, followers, and raw traffic are diagnostic only.

## Supply-first sequence

```text
provider research → qualification → CASL-aware outreach → interest
→ onboarding → required verification → listing activation
→ demand acquisition → successful transaction → review
→ repeat use → referral → greater local density
```

Research and contact may occur before verification. A provider cannot become eligible to transact or receive payments until required platform verification is complete.

## Expansion gate

```text
active micro-market
→ sufficient verified provider density and usable capacity
→ acceptable match rate and time to match
→ real completed transactions
→ demonstrated repeat usage
→ acceptable cancellation, dispute, fraud, and safety indicators
→ acceptable contribution economics
→ red-team review
→ founder decision on adjacent geography/category
```

Thresholds are set for each market before evaluation; they are never replaced with a calendar rule.

## Growth loops

### Provider distribution loop

Provider joins → creates a useful service/business page → shares it → local customers arrive → some become platform users → discover other providers → transact → create reviews/content → improve local discovery → attract more providers.

### Referral loop

Successful transaction → relevant post-value referral request/incentive → nearby user joins → local network density improves → marketplace quality and repeat utility improve. Referral prompts normally follow value delivery, not signup.

## Content and channel principles

Content targets local discovery, transactional intent, provider education, community/provider stories, customer proof, and local SEO. Programmatic pages require legitimate current supply and differentiated data; empty or thin location pages are not eligible. Outreach is targeted and personalized, never a mass-spam system.

## Economics

`AOV × platform take rate = gross platform revenue`. Subtract payment processing, refund loss, disputes, incentives, fraud, support, infrastructure allocation, and attributable acquisition cost to obtain contribution margin. MRR is not a primary metric unless a subscription product is explicitly evaluated.

## Decisions and governance

All material recommendations cite evidence, confidence, assumptions, gate results, and owners. Important launch, expansion, offer, automation, or spending decisions require [`red-team-review`](../.agents/skills/red-team-review/SKILL.md). Operating details are in [MARKETING-OPERATING-MODEL.md](MARKETING-OPERATING-MODEL.md).

# GTM Strategy: Density Before Expansion

## Objective

Build a trusted, liquid local marketplace one geography × category micro-market at a time. Downloads, registrations, impressions, followers, and raw traffic are diagnostic metrics, not the north star.

## Strategic sequence

`candidate research → weighted selection → supply qualification → compliant outreach → provider interest → onboarding → verification → listing activation → demand activation → completed transaction → review → repeat → referral → density`

Provider research and contact may precede verification. No provider becomes eligible to transact or receive payments until required platform verification is complete.

## North-star hierarchy

1. Successful local matches and completed local transactions.
2. Match rate, search-to-booking conversion, time to match, and liquidity by geography/category.
3. Active provider density, supply utilization, repeat customer/provider rates, and referral rate.
4. GMV and contribution margin, with cancellation, dispute, refund, and fraud indicators as guardrails.

## Expansion gate

Expansion is considered only when the active micro-market demonstrates founder-approved levels of provider density, match rate, completed transactions, repeat usage, time to match, trust/safety health, and contribution economics. Thresholds must be defined before evaluation and supported by a sufficient measurement window. Passing a calendar milestone is never sufficient.

## Supply-first operating choice

Targeted provider acquisition precedes scaled demand. Qualification, personalization, and CASL review are mandatory; mass spam is prohibited. Activated supply means verified providers with legitimate, bookable inventory—not scraped names or incomplete profiles.

## Content and distribution

Content should strengthen local discovery, transactional intent, provider education, community/provider stories, customer proof, and legitimate local SEO. Programmatic pages require real supply/data in that geography/category; thin or empty pages must not be published.

## Economics

Marketplace revenue is `average order value × take rate`. Contribution margin subtracts payment processing, refund losses, disputes, incentives, fraud loss, support, infrastructure allocation, and attributable acquisition cost. MRR is not the primary KPI unless a subscription product is explicitly evaluated.

## Decision controls

Research and internal drafts are autonomous. Legal, safety, and money/action gates in [the operating model](MARKETING-OPERATING-MODEL.md) control external execution. Important recommendations require [Red Team review](../.agents/skills/red-team-review/SKILL.md).
